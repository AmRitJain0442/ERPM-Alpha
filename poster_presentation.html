1. Problem

Why the Indian Market Is Hard to Predict

  • USD/INR is a managed-float market shaped by RBI intervention, oil shocks, dollar cycles, and delayed news absorption.
  • Raw sentiment is weak: Goldstein vs IMF3 = 0.010; US average tone vs USD/INR = 0.164.
  • Delayed effects still appear: Goldstein peaks at 9 days and tone at 12 days.
Key insight: the market looks random in raw aggregates, but delayed and theme-specific structure appears after filtering.
2. VMD + Stats

VMD Reframes the Task from Price to Volatility

  • Three-mode VMD targets IMF3, the high-frequency and news-sensitive part of USD/INR.
  • Phase B uses four themes: Economy, Conflict, Policy, Corporate.
  • Tone_Economy = -0.147 with IMF3; the Phase-C danger-day classifier reaches 40% precision and 52% recall.
3. Monte Carlo

Monte Carlo Supports a Noise-Dominant Market View

  • Monte Carlo is most useful as a distributional risk engine, not a single-point predictor.
  • Regime switching is the most balanced 30-day setup: median 90.370, std 1.336, 5th to 95th percentile 88.232 to 92.618.

5. TEPC

Most Recent Research: Topology-Enabled Predictions from Chaos

  • TEPC treats USD/INR as one node in a macro-geopolitical network containing DXY, BRENT, GOLD, rates, sentiment, and Goldstein pressure.
  • Rolling correlations define the graph; Laplacian summaries and coupled Lorenz chaos features become predictors.
  • tepc_full wins the strict raw-GDELT run: 77.5% breakout accuracy, 0.337 macro-F1, 0.002746 MAE return. Live runs also show chaos_only = 80% breakout accuracy.

Final Message: the Indian FX market is not solved by a bigger black-box model. The strongest repo path is a layered pipeline: VMD + thematic GDELT filtering + probabilistic checks + bounded multi-agent reasoning + TEPC topology/chaos features.
